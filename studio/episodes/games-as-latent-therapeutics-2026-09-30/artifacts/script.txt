Tetris was entertainment. Then controlled experiments deployed it after trauma exposure or memory reminders and reported fewer intrusive memories under specific protocols. The hypothesis is elegant: a visuospatial task competes for working-memory resources used by intrusive imagery. Someone hid a research instrument inside falling blocks. A mechanic can be clinically relevant without the game being a treatment: practice is not transfer, and fewer intrusive memories is not curing PTSD. Now widen the screen. League is an attention stress test. MMO raids are middle management with dragons. RPGs industrialize goals, rewards, progression and mastery. Screen the mechanics: match each to a function; test timing, dose, transfer and harms. Familiar interfaces may also engage men whom health tools routinely bore. Gaming’s masterpiece is adherence. It persuaded people to defeat the same digital dragon four hundred times for a hat.
