What if a mental-health tool is hiding inside an old puzzle game—not because Tetris is medicine, but because it may engage the right system at the right moment? Controlled trauma studies have reportedly found fewer intrusive memories when Tetris followed specific trauma-exposure or memory-reminder procedures. Researchers propose visuospatial interference. That mechanism is plausible, not settled. Fewer intrusions is not preventing PTSD. And ordinary play is not the protocol. The larger opportunity is Steam as a screening library. Games repeatedly demand attention, planning, spatial processing, coordination and goal pursuit. That generates hypotheses—not therapies. Because practice may only improve play. Players self-select. Benefits may not transfer, while sleep loss, compulsion, toxicity and avoidance may.
