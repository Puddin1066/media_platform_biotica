A patent is a very expensive permission slip. An orthotic that arrives tomorrow is a product. Northeastern’s CRI says InLoop is developing custom orthotics made in a podiatrist’s office with 3D printing and looping metal foam—claimed turnaround: 24 hours. That turns painful feet, training, work, aging—the whole mobility mess—into an overnight manufacturing problem. These are commercialization and development milestones, not proof of clinical validation, broad availability, or durable sales. CRI reported nine new companies, 188 disclosures, 333 patent applications, 66 granted patents, 12 executed licenses or options, and $25.7 million in additional spinout fundraising for FY2026. Lovely scoreboard. But scoreboards do not buy shoes. Since 2011, CRI reports 122 research-based companies. Sixty-one are active; nine were acquired. Starting is an event. Surviving is a business model. The university is not selling inventions. It is trying to manufacture companies durable enough to make inventions ordinary.
