A patent is a very expensive permission slip. An orthotic delivered tomorrow is a product. Northeastern’s CRI says InLoop is developing custom orthotics in a podiatrist’s office: 3D printing, looping metal foam, claimed turnaround of 24 hours. Which is a much better idea than making someone wait weeks while their foot continues its hostile takeover. These are CRI-reported commercialization and development milestones—not proof of clinical validation, broad availability, or durable demand. The scoreboard is busy: 188 disclosures, 333 patent applications, 66 granted patents, 12 licenses or options, nine new companies, and $25.7 million in additional spinout fundraising for FY2026. But the adult number is cumulative: 122 companies built on Northeastern research since 2011; 61 listed active. Starting a company is an event. Surviving is a business model. The pipeline runs from orthotics to microbe therapies, compressed AI, network automation, and thermal management. Fine. A university is not selling inventions. It is trying to manufacture companies durable enough to make the inventions matter.
