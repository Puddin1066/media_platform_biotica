A patent is a very expensive permission slip. An orthotic made tomorrow is a product. Northeastern’s CRI says InLoop is developing a podiatrist-office workflow: 3D printing, looping-coil metal foam, custom orthotics in 24 hours. That turns sore feet, training, work, aging—the whole tedious mobility economy—into a manufacturing problem. Neat trick, if real. These are CRI-reported development and commercialization milestones, not proof of clinical benefit, broad availability, or durability. The FY2026 scoreboard: 188 disclosures, 333 patent applications, 66 patents, 12 licenses or options, nine new companies, and $25.7 million in additional spinout fundraising. But the adult number is cumulative: 122 companies built on Northeastern research since 2011; 61 active, nine acquired. Starting is an event. Survival is a business model. The pipeline runs from microbes to compressed AI to thermal management. Fine. The university isn’t selling inventions; it’s trying to manufacture companies durable enough to make the patent the least interesting thing about them.
