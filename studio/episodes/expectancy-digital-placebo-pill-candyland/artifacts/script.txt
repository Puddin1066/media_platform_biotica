In 2016, Cláudia Carvalho’s team gave people with chronic low-back pain pills they were explicitly told had no active ingredient. Three weeks later, the group taking those pills alongside usual care reported larger reductions in pain and disability than usual care alone. That’s the strange part. Not a hidden sugar pill. An announced one. So what was delivered? Explanation. Timing. Repetition. Attention. The tiny ceremonial moment where care officially begins. Maybe the pill is not the treatment. Maybe it is the interface. A phone can explain a protocol, ring at the right time, repeat the rationale, and ask how you feel. It cannot become a molecule. But it can be a very competent stage manager. Whether that digital ritual can reproduce a pill protocol’s effects is untested. Which makes shipping inert objects slightly hilarious: perhaps we have been mailing a physical user interface through the postal system.
